# YouTube Content Plan — "Before & After: A Peptide Journey"

## 1. The Story (Your Unfair Advantage)

Most peptide/GLP-1 channels are run by doctors, influencers, or people 6 months into their journey. You have something none of them have: **a 30-year arc with a definitive ending.**

The timeline that anchors everything:

| Year | Milestone |
|------|-----------|
| Dec 1993 | Arrive in the US from France — 5'7", 178 lbs |
| Mid-late 1990s | Now 5'9", over 200 lbs — the American lifestyle catches up |
| 1990s–2020s | 30+ years of yo-yo dieting: lose, regain, repeat — eventually peaking at 300+ lbs |
| Recent | Tirzepatide, then retatrutide — the rollercoaster finally stops |
| Today | **192 lbs at 12.5% body fat** |

That's a **110+ lb loss**, ending not just "thinner" but genuinely *lean* — 12.5% body fat is athlete territory, which almost nobody on GLP-1 medications achieves. That's your differentiator: **most people on these medications lose weight; you got lean.** The muscle-preservation angle (protein, resistance training, dosing strategy) is a content goldmine because it answers the #1 fear about these drugs ("you'll just get skinny-fat and lose your muscle").

The French immigrant angle is also unique: you have a literal before/after of two food cultures. "I was never fat in France" is a hook that writes itself.

## 2. Channel Positioning

**One-line promise:** *"I fought my weight for 30 years and lost every time — until I didn't. Here's exactly what worked, what it cost, and what nobody tells you."*

**Tone:** Honest, specific, a little self-deprecating. Not a guru. Not selling anything. The guy at the end of the journey telling you what the map actually looks like.

**Target audience (in order):**
1. People 40+ who have yo-yo dieted for decades and are considering GLP-1 medications
2. People already on tirzepatide/semaglutide who are losing muscle or stalling and want to finish lean
3. People curious/skeptical about peptides who want a real person's long-term account

## 3. Content Pillars

Every video fits one of four pillars:

### Pillar A — The Journey (story-driven, broad appeal)
The emotional transformation content. Best for channel growth and new-viewer acquisition.
- The full 30-year story
- Before/after photo timelines
- "What being 300+ lbs actually felt like" (clothes, airplane seats, doctors, energy)
- The France → America food culture shock

### Pillar B — The Protocol (practical, search-driven)
What you actually did. This is what search traffic finds.
- Tirzepatide experience: month-by-month, side effects, plateaus
- Switching from tirzepatide to retatrutide: why, what changed
- How you hit 12.5% body fat instead of getting "skinny-fat": protein targets, resistance training, dose management
- Food noise: what it was, what silence sounds like
- Cost, sourcing realities, working with providers

### Pillar C — The Rollercoaster Autopsy (credibility + relatability)
Why 30 years of dieting failed. This separates you from every "I lost weight in 8 months" channel.
- Every diet you tried and why each one worked... temporarily
- The regain pattern: what actually triggers it
- Why willpower was never the problem (the biology argument, from lived experience)
- What you'd tell your 1995 self

### Pillar D — Maintenance & Life After (retention, long-term community)
The chapter nobody covers because nobody's there yet.
- Maintaining at 192: what changes, what doesn't
- Do you stay on medication forever? Your actual plan
- Body image after massive loss (loose skin, seeing yourself differently)
- Fitness goals now that weight isn't the goal

## 6. Titles & Thumbnails

**Thumbnail formula that works for transformation content:**
- Split-frame before/after with your face clearly visible in both — faces drive clicks
- Big number as text overlay: "300 → 192" or "-110 lbs" (numbers outperform words)
- Consistent visual style so returning viewers recognize you in the feed

**Title rules:**
- Lead with the specific number or timespan: "30 years", "110 lbs", "12.5%"
- First-person and past-tense ("I finally...") outperforms advice-framing ("How to...") for journey content; use "How I..." for Pillar B protocol videos
- Never clickbait beyond what the video delivers — transformation audiences punish it hard in retention

## 7. Production Notes (Keep It Simple)

- **Camera:** a modern phone is genuinely enough. Prioritize a $20 lavalier/USB mic — audio quality matters more than video quality.
- **Setting:** one consistent, well-lit spot (window light works). Consistency = brand.
- **B-roll that costs nothing:** old photos, old clothes (the 4XL shirt hold-up is a classic for a reason), the tape measure, meal prep, gym clips.
- **Edit style:** cut every pause. Talking-head + photo overlays is all this niche needs. CapCut or DaVinci Resolve (both free).
- **Batch:** film 2 videos per session while energy is high.

## 8. Compliance & Safety (Important — Read Before Publishing)

This niche has real content-policy and legal exposure. Protecting the channel from day one:

1. **Verbal + on-screen disclaimer in every medication video:** "I'm not a doctor. This is my personal experience, not medical advice. Talk to your physician." Also put it in every description.
2. **Retatrutide is investigational.** It is not FDA-approved and is only legitimately available in clinical trials. Say this on camera whenever you discuss it. Frame everything as personal experience under medical circumstances — never as a recommendation.
3. **Never tell viewers where to buy anything**, never share sourcing for non-approved compounds, and never give dosing advice ("ask your doctor what's right for you"). This is the line between "personal journey channel" and "channel that gets terminated / creates real liability."
4. **YouTube monetization:** personal-experience medical content is generally fine; content that could be read as promoting unapproved drugs is not. The story-first framing (Pillars A/C/D) is your safest growth engine; keep Pillar B experiential.
5. **Be honest about the hard parts** — side effects, cost, the possibility of regain if medication stops. It's ethically right and it's also what makes the channel trustworthy and different.

## 9. Metrics That Matter (First 6 Months)

- **Retention on the flagship video** (target: 50%+ at 30 seconds, 35%+ average) — this tells you if the storytelling works
- **Click-through rate** (target: 4–6%) — this tells you if thumbnails/titles work
- Returning viewers trending up month-over-month — this tells you if you're building an audience, not just getting views
- Ignore subscriber count for 90 days; it lags everything else

2. **12.5% body fat is the hook within the hook.** "Lost weight on GLP-1s" is common. "Finished lean" is not.
3. **30 years of failure is the credibility.** Every viewer who has regained weight sees themselves in you. You're not a genetic outlier or a 25-year-old — you're proof for the people who have tried everything.
